About this site and how it is built

This site answers one question - what to wear to the event you are going to - and stops there on purpose. No trend reports, no seasonal color forecasts, no outfit of the day.

Each page is built from three things: a rule (what the venue, the dress code or the room requires), concrete data (lengths, shoes, layers, what the building allows in, the color that keeps you out of the frame), and a decision you can check in the try-on before you buy. The photos are staged the same way every time: the same garment, the same light, the same framing, so a difference you can see is a difference in the outfit and not in the shoot.

What this site does not do: no affiliate links inside the guidance, no paid placement, no measuring of your body. If a page needs a number it does not have, the block is removed rather than invented. Where a policy varies by courthouse, venue or employer, the page says so and tells you which office to check.

Fit advice by body type, size or age is deliberately absent. That is a different question with different rules, and mixing it into an occasion guide is how both get answered badly.

Who writes it. Two people: one writes and checks the dress-code and venue rules, the other builds and maintains the try-on app. No agency, no influencer roster.

Where the rules come from

Invitation wording, published venue and courthouse policies, and the practical constraints of events that last hours. Anything that varies locally is marked as varying.

How the photos are made

One garment, one light setup, one framing, then the crop changes only who is wearing it. If the light changes, the shot is redone.

Your photo

The photo you upload is used to generate your result. It is not shared with brands and not used for advertising.

Questions

Do you get paid to recommend anything?

No. There are no affiliate links in the guidance and no commission on a color or a brand. The only thing sold here is the try-on app.

Why is there nothing about body type?

Because an occasion guide and a fit guide answer different questions, and we would rather do one of them properly. The event pages hold to the room, the hours and the audience.

Can I ask for a correction?

Yes. If a rule here is wrong for your venue, employer or region, tell us: we check it, fix it and move the review date on the page.

If you came here looking for a rule, there is one: the host's instruction beats anything on this site.

Now see it on you

What you just read applies to your own body and coloring. Upload a photo and see the piece on you, not on a model: it is the only test that settles it.
