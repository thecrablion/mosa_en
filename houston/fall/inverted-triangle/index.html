The hood is for the sidewalk, not the desk

From the car to the door, the hood earns its place. October’s normal is 5.46 inches, and a single storm can soak a collar. Once there is a roof, the hood comes down. Left up, with October’s 4.35 kWh/m²/day between storms, it throws shade on both sides of the neck and the V doubles. This jacket has no epaulette and no tab at the shoulder. A little ease sits at the hip: a straight leg with a trace of room, not a bell that drags.

The 28-inch crop is not lent to 32

28-inch inseam

A pant that shows the ankle takes a block off the shoulder. The crop works if the hem stays out of the puddle, not in it. An ankle-length shell at this inseam covers the leg, and the body becomes a rectangle with shoulders.

32-inch regular

That same crop looks short, and the shoulder wins again. At 32 inches the hem goes to the ankle, not to a bare shin. The 5'4" woman in the frames sits between the two lengths. She does not decide both.

September, at 90.4 °F and 5.11 kWh/m²/day, the hood is used for a shorter walk and the top is still the problem: a black top in that sun also heats the shoulder. November’s high is 72.6 °F and the sunlight is down to 3.23 kWh/m²/day. The epaulette still does not enter. A quieter top matters more when the light is flatter, not less.

Questions

Can the hood stay up if the shoulder is already wide?

On the walk, yes. Once there is a roof, no. October sunlight at the airport point averages 4.35 kWh/m²/day. A hood left up in that haze adds shoulder.

Does a cropped pant work at every inseam?

At 28 inches, a hem that shows the ankle balances the shoulder. At 32 inches that crop looks short and the shoulder leads more. At 32 the hem goes to the ankle.

What top fails on a cool undertone?

Black, in October haze. The shoulder reads heavier. The top goes to stone and the color drops to the pant.
