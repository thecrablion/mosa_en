What to wear to a job interview

The rule is one step above what you would wear on a normal day there. Not two. Two reads as a costume.

Interview dressing has one reliable rule: one step above the daily dress code of the place you are walking into. A tailored blazer over the usual t-shirt level for a tech company. A suit for a bank. A clean, ironed shirt and pressed chinos for retail management. Two steps up is how you end up looking like a candidate from another company.

The part that is often neglected is the camera. If any round is a video call, thin stripes and dense small prints cause shimmer on webcams, and pure white blows out. Solid mid-tone colors in navy, charcoal, forest, burgundy or slate read clean, and they keep you visible against most walls.

Then there are three fit checks that decide more than brand: the shoulder seam sits on the bone, the sleeve ends at the wrist bone, and the hem does not pool. Fix those three and almost any outfit reads as deliberate.

By industry

Finance, law, consulting: suit, closed toe, minimal jewelry. Tech and startups: blazer over a clean knit, dark denim or tailored trousers, clean sneakers are usually fine. Creative: one deliberate statement piece and quiet everything else. Healthcare, education, retail: practical, pressed, closed toe, nothing that limits movement.

On camera

Avoid thin stripes, small dense prints, and pure white. Solid navy, charcoal, forest, burgundy and slate stay clean on webcams and do not strobe. Keep contrast between you and your background: a mid-tone top against a light wall, or a light top against a dark one.

The three fit checks

Shoulder seam on the bone. Sleeve at the wrist bone. Hem not pooling. Those three fixable points beat a more expensive garment that misses all three.

  • Closed toe
  • Neutral bag, no logo wall
  • Hair and nails tidy and out of the way

  • Sneakers unless the culture wears them
  • Brand-new heels you have not walked in
  • Two statement pieces at once

Questions

Do I have to wear a suit even if no one there does?

No. Dress one step above their daily level. If the team wears t-shirts and jeans, a blazer over a clean knit is the top of the range, not a suit.

What about a video interview from home?

Neutral, uncluttered background, solid mid-tone top, and check that your camera angle is at eye level. Lighting in front of you, not behind: a window behind you turns your face into a silhouette.

What if the interview is right after work?

Bring the jacket, keep the base layer simple, and swap the shoes. A blazer over what you already wore that day handles most after-work interviews.

What do I wear to a job interview in summer?

The same colors as in winter, one fabric weight lighter: a short-sleeve blouse or a knit under a blazer you can put on in the lobby. Summer risk is sweat and a wrinkled jacket, so pick a fabric that hides both and carry the blazer until you are inside the air conditioning.

Do I need to wear a dress or a skirt?

No. Trousers and a structured top are a complete interview outfit, and for most industries they read as more current than a skirt. What the industry actually checks is covered shoulders, a closed shoe, no scent and nothing you have to adjust while you sit.

Prepare two levels, not one: the outfit for the panel and the version you would wear if they invite you to stay for an office tour. Same base, one layer different.
