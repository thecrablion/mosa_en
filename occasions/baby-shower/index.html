What to wear to a baby shower, and to a bridal shower

Two showers, two different traps: one is about not outshining, the other is about not wearing the color of the other guest of honor.

A shower is a two-to-four-hour daytime event in somebody's home, a restaurant private room or a back garden, where you will sit, eat standing up, help with games and end up in a group photograph. That combination - daytime, domestic, photographed - is the whole brief.

For a baby shower the outfit should not compete with the mother-to-be, but she is usually in something floaty and pale, so color is free: sage, terracotta, navy, dusty rose, cream-with-pattern. For a bridal shower, the rule tightens: no white, no ivory, no champagne, because that color belongs to the bride at any event where she is present and photographed.

The other decision people make too late is the shoe and the venue. A backyard in May and a restaurant in December want different things: grass means block heel or clean sneaker, a restaurant means you will be on a hard floor for two hours, and a home means you might end up on your hands and knees helping with a gift table.

What this page covers. Guest outfits by setting and season, what the mom-to-be should plan around (outfit and nursing access), the color list to skip for a bridal shower, and a table that separates the two events.

The guest version

A midi or knee dress in a solid color, a knit or a blouse with trousers. Skip the strapless and the very short: you will be leaning over a table. One earring and a watch is enough - the day belongs to somebody else.

If you are the guest of honor

Plan around the photographs and the three hours of standing, then around feeding. A dress with a zip or buttons at the front, or a top under everything, saves a wardrobe malfunction in the middle of a game. Buy for the shape you have on the day, not the one you have today.

Games and the gift table

Shower games involve sitting on the floor, writing on cards and carrying things. Skirts that sit, a bag you can set down, and shoes you can stand in for ninety minutes at a stretch. If there is a present-opening, you will be photographed holding a box.

Yes and no

  • Color you would not wear to a wedding
  • Midi or knee length
  • Shoes for standing on hard floors
  • A layer for the drive there
  • Slightly dressed-up, not dressed-for-a-wedding
  • Hair you can fix in a bathroom mirror
  • White, ivory or champagne at a bridal shower
  • All-white at a baby shower if the mom-to-be is in white
  • Sequins at an afternoon event
  • Stilettos on grass
  • A dress you cannot sit down in
  • Perfume you will wear for three hours indoors

Questions

What do you wear to a baby shower as a female guest?

A dress or a blouse-and-trousers combination in a color, mid-length, with flat or low shoes. If the invitation says nothing, aim for smart-casual daytime and add one accessory rather than three.

Is it okay to wear black to a baby shower?

Yes, with a color or texture that keeps it daytime: a knit, a print scarf, gold earrings. An all-black evening look reads as a different event, not as a mistake.

Why can't I wear white to a bridal shower?

Because the bride will be photographed and guests will compare. Even if she is not wearing white on the day, the color carries the wedding signal; you are not the one being honored.

What if the theme is 'he said yes' or color-coded by gender?

Follow the theme in a small way - a ribbon, an earring, a card - not head to toe. Nobody remembers your outfit; they remember the photograph you are in.

Do I bring a gift with the outfit I chose?

Irrelevant to the dress code, but plan for it: a small bag that fits a wrapped box. It is the most common practical failure at a shower.

Dress daytime, mid-length, in a color, in shoes you can stand in. Then check one thing on the invitation: whose shower it is - that decides whether white is off the table.
