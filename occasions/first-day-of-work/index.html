What to wear on the first day, and how to read the code

The interview outfit was meant to stand out. The first day is the opposite: dress like somebody who already belongs there.

The first day has one job: to be unremarkable in a room whose rules you have not seen yet. That is why the interview outfit is the wrong starting point - it was built to be noticed, and on day one being noticed is not the goal. Aim one notch below the most dressed-up person who is not the boss, and you land right in most US offices.

Since you cannot preview the office, get the information two other ways: ask the recruiter or the hiring manager plainly (*is the code business casual, and does that mean jeans?*), and look at the team's photos and the building's street view. Both take five minutes and both beat guessing. If nobody answers, wear the safe version: trousers or a midi skirt, a covered top, a jacket you can take off, closed shoes.

Then plan for the second week, not the first. After five days you will know whether heels are silly there, whether anyone wears jeans on Friday, and whether the building is cold. That is when you start buying - with the try-on, so you check the color and the length on you before anything goes in the cart.

Ask two questions, not one

Is it business casual or business formal? and does that include jeans or sneakers? Recruiting emails often say 'business casual' to mean 'whatever you wore to the interview, minus the suit'. A one-line question costs nothing and prevents the worst version of day one.

The safe baseline

Navy, charcoal, camel, gray or black trousers or a midi skirt, a solid top that does not need a camisole, a blazer or a knit over it, closed shoes with a low heel, a watch or one earring. Nothing new against your skin: you will be nervous and warm.

Buy in week two

The first week is reconnaissance. By day five you know the temperature, whether anyone wears jeans on Friday, and whether a jacket is actually worn or hung on a chair. Buy against what you saw, not against the job posting.

Questions

What is business casual for a woman, exactly?

In most US offices: trousers, a midi skirt or a dress with sleeves, a blouse, knit or polished shirt, and closed shoes - no tie required, no suit jacket mandatory. If two people in the same office disagree about the phrase, the more conservative reading is safer on day one.

Are jeans OK on a first day?

Not as a guess. Dark, clean, straight-leg jeans with a blouse and a jacket work in tech and creative offices; in a law firm they are a first-day story you do not want. Ask, and if you cannot ask, wear trousers.

Can I wear sneakers?

In tech, retail and hospitals, often yes: clean, minimal, not gym shoes. In client-facing roles, closed flats or loafers on day one and sneakers once you have seen other people wearing them.

Do I need a different outfit every day of the first week?

No. Five days from seven pieces is enough: two bottoms, three tops, one jacket, one cardigan, two pairs of shoes. Change the accessory and the layer, not the wardrobe.

What if I arrive and everyone is dressed down?

Then you have one day of being slightly overdressed, which is the safest failure mode available. Take the jacket off, remove an earring, and match the room from day two.

Wear the safe baseline, ask the two questions, and hold the shopping until week two. Day one is not the day to look your best; it is the day to learn what best means in that building.
